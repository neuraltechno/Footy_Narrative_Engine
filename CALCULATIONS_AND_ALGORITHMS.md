# Footy Narrative Engine: Calculations & Algorithms Reference

This document provides a concise paragraph definition for every major calculation, algorithm, and metric implemented within the R analytics engine (`engine/modules/`) of the Footy Narrative Engine.

## 1. Player Impact Rating (PIR) & Metrics (`10_player_metrics.R`)
The Player Impact Rating (PIR) is a comprehensive single-game performance score designed to quantify a player's total net contribution. It aggregates positive contributions across five distinct performance categories—Disposal & Ball Use (`cat_disposal`), Contested Play & Clearances (`cat_contest_clearance`), Damaging Scoring Impact (`cat_damaging_impact`), Defensive Pressure & Grit (`cat_defensive_grit`), and Ruck Supremacy (`cat_ruck`)—each multiplied by tuned weights configured in `00_config.R`. Mistakes (such as clangers, free kicks conceded, and lost defensive one-on-ones) are summed into raw penalty points (`raw_mistake_points`), divided by total player actions to determine a mistake rate, and non-linearly dampened to form `PIR_Negative`. A Time-on-Ground (TOG) modifier gently scales up scores for players with less than 80% game time to account for partial appearances, before subtracting the negative penalty from the adjusted positive score to yield the final net `PIR`.

## 2. Season Aggregation & Timeline Grid Completion (`20_season_aggregation.R`)
The Season Aggregation engine builds player-level season profiles and standardizes historical tracking. It completes a full round-by-round timeline grid (`complete()`) for every player across all rounds in the season bounds (`min_rd` to `max_rd`), converting missing rounds into unplayed filler rows. It computes running sums, running averages (requiring at least 3 games), and weekly player rank positions based on rolling performance. Additionally, it compiles season-level player bios and computes **Significant Strengths** by comparing a player's average category output against their specific positional group mean (`league_category_means`), surfacing top differentiating skills. Finally, it measures week-over-week `Rank_Delta` to tag player trends (`up`, `down`, `stable`).

## 3. Player Advanced Metrics & Breakout Watch (`15_player_advanced_metrics.R`)
This module drives player-centric leaderboards, including the **Breakout Watch**, **Category Kings**, **Top Games**, and the **Expected Score Contribution (ESC)** leaderboard. The Breakout Watch identifies emerging young talent (players aged 23 or younger, or with fewer than 30 career games) by evaluating their recent 3-round average PIR against their season average (Form Delta). It weights this delta using an age-taper slope and sample confidence multiplier to surface surging prospects within each position group. Category Kings tracks positional leaderboards across disposal, clearance, damage, grit, and ruck metrics, maintaining week-over-week snapshots to calculate streak lengths and rank movements. The ESC leaderboard calculates an alternative offensive attribution metric weighting goals, goal assists, score launches, inside 50s, clearances, and meters gained while penalizing turnovers.

## 4. Team Metrics Engine (`30_team_metrics.R`)
The Team Metrics engine aggregates individual player match-by-match performances into cohesive team-level structural outputs for every round. It groups player records by team and structural line—**Engine Room** (Midfielders and Rucks), **Iron Curtain** (Backs), and **The Arsenal** (Forwards)—summing their PIR contributions per line. Simultaneously, it pulls real disposal totals directly from played match data (`player_round_stats`), joining them by round and team. It calculates **System Velocity** by dividing total team player PIR by actual round disposals, capturing output efficiency per possession, and determines the **Overall Rating** as the mean player baseline match score (total PIR divided by 22).

## 5. Match Engine & Expected Scores (`40_match_metrics.R`)
The Match Engine processes match results, converting raw scoring events into an **Expected Score (`xscore`)** baseline using historical goal-and-behind weighting coefficients (`XSCORE_W_*`). By evaluating how a team's scoring shots translate through this weighted baseline, the engine identifies the statistical `expected_winner` and compares it against the `actual_winner`. A match is flagged as a **Robbery** (`is_robbery`) when a definitive winner contradicts the model's expected winner, with the magnitude of disagreement quantified by the absolute `luck_delta`. When quarter-by-quarter score-worm data is available (`quarter_scores`), the engine builds a per-quarter timeline tracking cumulative scores, lead changes, come-from-behind wins, and the biggest deficits overcome or leads surrendered.

## 6. Justice Ladder & Probabilistic Standings (`50_justice_ladder.R`)
The Justice Ladder ranks teams by their true football performance rather than raw scoreboard luck, converting expected score differentials into win, loss, and draw probabilities using a normal-CDF win model with a Gaussian draw bump (`compute_win_probabilities`). Teams accumulate **Expected Points** ($4 \times \text{Win Prob} + 2 \times \text{Draw Prob}$) alongside actual competition points, defining a **Luck Rating** ($\text{Actual Points} - \text{Expected Points}$) where positive values indicate good fortune. A Pythagorean cross-check evaluates scoring-based expected wins to separate model misjudgment from margin luck, while rolling window metrics, home/away splits, and strength-of-schedule proxies combine to tag teams with status descriptors such as "Snakebitten", "Riding the breaks", or "Buried by others' luck".

## 7. Power Rankings / The Form Pulse (`60_power_rankings.R`)
The Form Pulse module calculates a rolling power score for each team to measure current output quality independent of season-long standing. It averages overall ratings and system velocities across a trailing window of rounds (defaulting to 3), and applies an **Opponent Strength Adjustment** by scaling the raw power score against the average season strength of the opponents faced during that window. Teams beating above-average opposition receive an upward adjustment, while those facing easier competition are discounted. Finally, `trend` is derived by comparing each team's current power rank against their rank in the previous round, categorizing them as **Rising**, **Falling**, or **Steady**.

## 8. Narratives & JSON Export (`90_narratives.R`, `95_narrative_copy.R`, `99_export_json.R`)
The narrative and export modules consume the calculated metrics and statistical frames to synthesize human-readable story hooks and persist structured JSON data structures. The narrative engine evaluates statistical anomalies—such as extreme luck ratings, home/road splits, rating-versus-ladder divergences, and major comebacks—against configuration thresholds (`NARRATIVE_*`) to generate automated editorial copy. Finally, the export engine structures team metrics, match centers, ladder snapshots, and leaderboards into ordered JSON files (`json/`) ready to power the frontend interface.
